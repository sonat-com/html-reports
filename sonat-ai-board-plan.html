A 14-week plan to make AI the strongest thing we sell — and to be found for it
Sonat leadership · 31 July 2026 · Covering 3 August – 6 November 2026 · Decisions requested in section 10

1  The ask, in one paragraph

We have an AI answer engine that is refusing 81% of the questions customers ask it, and a marketing site that promises things the product does not do. We also have the one asset no competitor in this category has: we own both the documentation tool and the answer engine. This paper asks the board to approve fourteen weeks of work that fixes the engine, turns it into a measurable product, and makes Sonat the answer when a buyer — or a buyer's AI assistant — asks who does this. It also asks for a hard rule: we do not market or price this until the engine is measurably fixed.

2  The strategic position

2.1  What we are actually selling

The instinct was to sell “Enterprise gets the better AI model.” I am recommending against that, for four reasons that survived research:

  • It is copyable in a weekend. Every competitor buys models from the same three vendors and can switch backends by changing a string.
  • It goes stale by construction. Our pricing page today sells “AI Answer Generator with GPT‑4” — a model we have not offered for months.
  • It does not fix our actual problem. 85% of our refusals happen with the right documents retrieved. That is retrieval and prompting, not model capability.
  • It invites the one question we cannot answer: “if it's just GPT, why don't I paste my docs into ChatGPT?”
The defensible position instead: kapa.ai, Intercom Fin, Docsbot and Document360's Eddy all answer questions over documentation they do not own and cannot edit. They can tell a customer their docs have a hole. Only Sonat can fix the hole in the same tool — because we own the authoring app, the viewer, the search index and the answer engine. And the category's own benchmark says that loop is the variable that decides outcomes: 45% ticket deflection when the knowledge base was updated in the last 30 days, against 18% when it had not been touched in six months. Freshness beats model. Freshness is what we control and nobody else does.

2.2  So “Sonat AI” means four things, in this order

LayerWhat it isWhy it is defensible
1. Answers Cited, fast, multi-turn chat on the viewer and in the widget — and inside the customer's own Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor via our MCP server Table stakes in 2026. Not having chat is the gap.
2. Proof A dashboard showing every question asked, what was answered, what failed, and the deflection value in dollars The only AI feature that produces a number a buyer puts in a business case.
3. The loop Every question the docs could not answer becomes a one-click draft topic in the editor The moat. No pure-AI vendor can build it — they do not own the editor.
4. Control Scope, per-document exclusions, audit log, provider choice, no-training guarantee, DPA The procurement gate. Legal kills more deals in this category than features do.

Advanced model selection — which we shipped on 30 July — becomes a line item inside layer 4. It is a fine cost decision and a poor value story.

The wedge we are not using

A customer deflecting 500 tickets a month pays $500–1,000/mo for AI alone at Intercom or Zendesk prices. We charge $375 for everything, with no per-answer fee and no per-viewer fee. That headline — unlimited AI answers, unlimited viewers, one price — costs nothing to say and no competitor can match it.

The pricing headroom

Once chat and analytics ship and refusals are down, Enterprise supports $500–650/mo and still undercuts Document360's platform ($799) and kapa.ai's AI layer alone ($999). That is roughly 2× headroom before we reach market price — but only after the gate.

5  The 14-week plan

Week 0 — 3–7 AugustOne day of work. Can redirect the whole quarter.
Six questions we have never answered, each cheap: how many accounts even have AI switched on; how many Business accounts have ever converted to Enterprise; what plans the worst-affected accounts are on; whether our top tier is being wrongly blocked from the models it pays for; whether anyone is using the MCP tool yet; and what an answer actually costs us. In parallel and needing no answers: correct every false claim on the website.
Decision meeting Friday 7 August. Go / no-go for the quarter.
Phase 1 — Weeks 1–4 — Make it work~18 dev-days
Fix ranking so real content outranks placeholder text. Filter template stubs out of the AI's context. Rewrite the prompt so it answers partially instead of refusing wholesale. Make the search staleness check parallel and fail-open instead of silently deleting results. Reindex the broken account and alert on it happening again. Keep the answer service warm. And restore the analytics pipeline first — otherwise we ship blind and cannot prove any of it worked. Security fixes ride along, because the rate limiter we need for safety is the same one we will need for metering.
Exit gate: refusals under 20% on 3+ accounts · median answer under 5 seconds · no account at 100% · measured, not inferred, for 7 consecutive days. Nothing downstream starts until this passes.
Phase 2 — Weeks 5–8 — Make it provable~20 dev-days
Ship AI Insights: every question asked, answered versus failed, which topics were cited, the ranked list of questions the documentation could not answer, and a deflection estimate in dollars. Ship the exclude-from-AI flag and the audit log. Unblock the editor AI assistant (five pull requests have been open since 23 July). Begin SOC 2 Type 2 — it is a 6–12 month clock and Type 1 is no longer accepted by enterprise buyers.

And run the cheapest possible test of this entire thesis: hand-build a Documentation Performance Review for five Business accounts — their traffic, their top searches, the searches that returned nothing useful, and what that cost them in support tickets. We can build these today from BigQuery with no new engineering. If nobody replies, we do not build the rest.
Exit gate: 2+ replies from the 5 review pilots · Insights live for 3 accounts.
Phase 3 — Weeks 9–12 — Make it a product, and make it visible~33 dev-days
Sonat AI Chat on the viewer — multi-turn, cited, customer-branded, with escalation to support. Enterprise MCP — authenticated tokens so a customer's own Claude or ChatGPT can query their private manuals, with an audit trail. Something a public help centre cannot do. The content-gap loop — failed questions become one-click drafts. Then the launch: website, comparison pages, blog series, release notes, Product Hunt, and the visibility work in §6.
Exit gate: every public claim we make is provable on demand.
Phase 4 — Weeks 13–14 — Make it pricedCommercial only
Enterprise moves to $500–650, existing accounts grandfathered 12 months. Answer allowances enforced as cost control. Upgrade offers go to the Business base — carried by their own performance review, not a feature list. Enterprise Plus stays custom, differentiated by a CSM-led quarterly content-gap review: a service wrapper that costs us nothing to build and cannot be commoditised.

6  Being found — by Google and by the AI assistants

Buyers increasingly do not start on Google. They ask Claude, ChatGPT or Perplexity “what's the best AI documentation platform?” or “what are the alternatives to Document360?” Today those assistants do not name us — and the reason is structural, not reputational: there is no structured data anywhere on sonat.com, and no machine-readable description of what we do. We are asking models to infer us from prose.

7  The commercial reality, honestly

7.1  Converting the Business base has a low ceiling

BusinessEnterpriseStep
Entry price$40/mo$375/mo9.4×
Seats included515+10 unneeded
Per user$8$253.1×

Thirteen active Business accounts at $375 is ~$4.9k MRR — the entire theoretical ceiling of “convert our Business base.” And a five-person team cannot take that step: they must pay 9.4× and buy ten seats they will never use. We could build every feature in this plan and convert nobody, because the price step has no relationship to the value step.

Recommendation: let Enterprise start at five seats, around $125–175/mo, decoupling capability from seat count. The trade-off is real — we would earn less from customers who would have paid $375 — so this is gated on one Stripe query in Week 0. If Business → Enterprise conversions are near zero, 3× from a customer who converts beats 9.4× from one who never moves. If they are healthy, we leave pricing alone.

The larger prize is new logos and retention, not upsell. An enterprise-credible AI story is a win-rate and price-point lever on new business. And separately: 1,044 free accounts sit at 11–40 topics, pressed against the limit and not upgrading. AI will not move them — the topic cliff will. That is a different campaign and we should not conflate the two.

7.2  The number that could invalidate all of this

337 AI answers per month, platform-wide. About eleven a day, across every account we have. There are four possible explanations and they demand four different responses:
  • AI is switched off for most accounts → this is an onboarding and defaults problem. Cheapest win available to us.
  • It is on but nobody can find it → a viewer UI problem, not an AI problem.
  • Users tried it, got refused, and stopped → the engine fix is the whole job and demand follows.
  • End users do not search at all → the AI investment thesis is weak and we should spend this quarter elsewhere.

Week 0 answers this with a single database query. I want that answer before we commit the quarter, and I am flagging to the board that it is a genuine kill criterion rather than a formality.
