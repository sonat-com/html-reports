Executive summary

Spend rose modestly to $78.07 (+5.0% week-over-week), but the account bought less and costlier traffic for it. Link Clicks fell to 336 (−15.2%) while CPC (Link Click) climbed to $0.23 (+23.7%), and Link-Click CTR dropped by the same 15.2%.

Impressions held essentially flat (11,576, +0.0%) but Reach declined to 9,916 (−4.7%), so Frequency edged up to 1.17 (+4.9%) — the same volume of impressions delivered to fewer Accounts Center accounts. CPM ticked up to $6.74 (+5.0%). Broad-interaction quality softened too: CTR (all) −8.2% and Clicks (all) −8.2%.

This is a single traffic-objective campaign (LINK_CLICKS); no purchase or ROAS conversions are tracked, so efficiency here is judged on click cost and quality, not sales. The pattern — flat delivery, rising frequency, falling CTR — is the kind of mild early-fatigue signal that Meta's own two active recommendations (Advantage+ audience, mixed image/video formats) are designed to relieve.

Recommendations testable hypotheses, framed against overall performance

Hypothesis 1 · aligns with official rec

Test Advantage+ audience to relieve rising click costs

Evidence: CPC (Link Click) rose +23.7% and CPM +5.0% while Reach fell −4.7% at flat impressions — delivery is concentrating on fewer accounts. Mechanic: broadening the audience gives the auction more room to find cheaper marginal impressions. Expected impact: Meta's own estimate is ~9.7% lower median cost per result; watch overall CPC (Link Click) and Link Clicks, not just reach. Run it as a test, not a permanent switch.

Hypothesis 2 · aligns with official rec

Refresh creative with a video variant

Evidence: Link-Click CTR −15.2% and CTR (all) −8.2% alongside Frequency +4.9% — the same accounts seeing the ad more often and clicking less is a classic mild-fatigue shape. Mechanic: a new format resets what accounts see and can lift relevance. Expected impact: recovering CTR toward last week's 3.4% link-click rate would pull CPC (Link Click) back down at equal spend. Add the variant; keep the incumbent live so the comparison is clean.

Hypothesis 3 · measurement discipline

Don't cut budget on one soft week — confirm the trend first

Evidence: Every efficiency metric moved the wrong way this week, but this is a single 7-day aggregate with no day-by-day series. A higher average CPC (Link Click) alone is not grounds to reduce spend — it can reflect normal week-to-week variance. Action: hold spend steady and re-check next week; only treat two-plus consecutive weeks of decline as a real trend before any budget change. This deliberately avoids reacting to average cost in isolation.

Hypothesis 4 · tracking gap

Add conversion tracking if downstream results matter

Evidence: The window returned no Purchases / Purchase ROAS, and only 0 completed registrations this week vs 1 last week — the objective optimizes to Link Clicks, so quality past the click is invisible. Action: if signups or sales are the real goal, adding the relevant conversion event (or a conversion-objective test) would let the system optimize toward outcomes rather than clicks — and let future reports judge true efficiency. Framed as an option to evaluate, not a directive.

Methodology note

This is a weekly executive digest built from aggregate account- and campaign-level totals for the reporting window and the immediately preceding equal-length window (pulled from the Meta Graph API, v21.0). It reports what changed week-over-week. It intentionally does not include day-by-day time series, placement/audience/demographic breakdowns, or ad-set/ad-level data — so it does not attempt deep root-cause diagnosis (marginal-CPA / Breakdown-Effect analysis, learning-phase status, auction-overlap, or relevance-ranking diagnosis); the underlying data can't support those claims at this altitude. Audience figures are reported as "Accounts Center accounts." "Clicks (all)" denotes all interactions; "Link Clicks" denotes offsite link clicks. All money is in USD. Every figure is drawn directly from the fetched data; none is estimated.
