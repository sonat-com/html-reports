Executive summary

The account spent $76.04 this week (−3.3% WoW) on a single link-clicks (traffic) campaign and got more, cheaper traffic for less money: Link Clicks rose to 518 (+8.1%) while the CPC (Link Click) fell to $0.147 (−10.6%) and CTR (all) climbed to 5.16% (+7.0%). The creative is engaging a warmer share of the audience it reaches.

The trade-off was narrower delivery: Reach (Accounts Center accounts) fell to 11,568 (−9.3%) and Impressions to 12,918 (−6.4%), while CPM edged up to $5.89 (+3.3%) and Frequency ticked to 1.12 (+3.3%). Fewer accounts saw the ads, but those who did clicked through at a higher rate. This is a traffic-objective campaign, so purchases and Purchase ROAS are not tracked — efficiency is judged on clicks and cost-per-click, not sales.

Recommendations — testable hypotheses, not directives

1. Test Advantage+ Audience on the ad set Official Meta rec

Meta's own recommendation for this ad set estimates ~9.7% lower median cost per result from enabling Advantage+ Audience (opportunity score lift 3).

Why it fits this week: Reach fell −9.3% while Frequency rose, i.e. the ads are recirculating within a narrowing set of accounts. Letting the system look beyond the current audience bounds is a low-risk way to test whether it can recover reach and hold or lower CPC (Link Click). Keep location/age floors in place. Expected impact: wider fresh reach and potentially lower cost per link click overall.

2. Add a mix of image + video creative Official Meta rec

Meta recommends running a mix of image and video ads in the ad set (duplicate existing ads, swap in video, keep originals live).

Why it fits this week: engagement is the clear strength — CTR (all) +7.0% and Link Click CTR +15.5% — so the messaging is landing. Adding a format the current audience hasn't seen is a natural way to extend that resonance and reach new accounts as frequency creeps up, without touching what already works. Expected impact: incremental reach and sustained CTR; test at equal budget before shifting spend.

3. Test a modest budget increase while efficiency is favorable

This week delivered +8.1% more Link Clicks at −10.6% cheaper CPC (Link Click) on less spend — the campaign got more efficient, not less. That's the condition under which a cautious budget step-up (e.g. +15–20%) is worth testing to capture more clicks at today's favorable cost. Guardrail: watch CPC (Link Click) and Frequency for degradation as delivery scales; roll back if cost per click drifts up. No segment is being paused or cut here — this is an additive test.

4. Firm up conversion tracking before judging downstream results

The window recorded a single registration event and no purchase/ROAS signal. If registrations or calls are the real business goal, validating that the pixel / registration events fire reliably would let future digests report a trustworthy cost per registration. Until then, this campaign should be judged on the traffic metrics it does optimize for — Link Clicks, CTR, and CPC (Link Click) — all of which improved.

Methodology note

This is a weekly executive digest built from aggregate account- and campaign-level totals for the reporting window and the preceding equal-length window (week-over-week), pulled from the Meta Graph API (v21.0). It contains no day-by-day time series, no placement or audience breakdowns, and no ad-set / ad-level data. Accordingly it reports what changed and by how much — it does not attempt deep root-cause diagnosis (marginal-CPA / Breakdown-Effect, learning-phase status, auction-overlap, or relevance-ranking analysis), because the data to support those conclusions isn't present. Every figure is taken directly from the API response; metrics absent from the data (Purchases, Purchase ROAS) are reported as not tracked rather than estimated. Metric display names and the audience / clicks terminology follow the meta-ads-analyzer standards. Money is in USD.
